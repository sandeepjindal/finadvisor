# Earnings Season

How to think about a company reporting results — a high-information, high-volatility event.
The goal is to read the reaction, not to gamble on the number.

## Expectations, not absolutes
- Stocks trade on results **vs expectations**, not on whether numbers are "good." A record
  quarter can sell off if it missed what was priced in.
- **Consensus** is the published analyst estimate; the **whisper number** is the higher,
  unofficial bar the market actually expects. A beat on consensus but miss on the whisper
  often still drops the stock.
- Sentiment and price run-up going in *are* the expectations — a stock up 30% into the
  print has a much higher bar to clear.

## Guidance beats the headline EPS
- **Forward guidance usually moves the stock more than the reported quarter.** The past is
  known; the outlook is what gets repriced.
- A beat-and-raise (beat + raised guidance) is the strong combination; a beat with cut or
  withdrawn guidance is a warning, not a win.
- Read the whole picture: revenue vs EPS quality (one-offs, buybacks flattering EPS),
  margins, and management tone on the call.

## Options and IV crush
- Implied volatility (IV) inflates into earnings because a big move is expected. After the
  report, uncertainty resolves and **IV collapses — "IV crush"** (see `options_basics`).
- Buying a long call/put right before earnings often **loses even when direction is right**,
  because the premium you overpaid deflates faster than the move helps you.
- High IV rank into earnings favours **selling** premium (defined-risk spreads), not paying
  up for lottery tickets — but earnings-week short options carry real gap risk. Size tiny.

## Post-earnings drift
- Stocks that surprise strongly tend to keep drifting in the surprise's direction for weeks
  (post-earnings-announcement drift). The reaction is often the start of a trend, not the end.
- A gap up that holds and builds is more durable than a gap up that fades intraday — let
  the tape confirm before chasing.

## Avoid binary bets
- An earnings print is a coin-flip catalyst: the move can be large in either direction and
  is largely unpredictable. Betting the account on one report is speculation, not investing.
- If you hold through earnings, do it because the **long-term thesis** justifies it — sized
  so a bad gap is survivable (see `position_sizing`) — not for the pop.
- Prefer to let the dust settle: enter or add **after** the report, once the reaction and
  guidance are known, than to guess beforehand.

## Reading the reaction
- **Beat + rally that holds:** thesis confirmed, expectations were reasonable.
- **Beat + selloff:** expectations were too high, or guidance disappointed — respect it.
- **Miss + rally:** "less bad than feared," or a bullish outlook — sentiment was washed out.
- The *market's response* to the news is more informative than the news itself.
