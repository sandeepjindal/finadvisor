# Sector Rotation

How sectors take turns leading as the economy moves through its cycle. A lens for tilting,
not a timing machine — rotation is a probability, confirm it against real ETF trends.

## The cycle in four phases
- **Early cycle** (recovery off a bottom, rates falling/low, credit thawing): leaders are
  rate-sensitive and beaten-down cyclicals — **Financials (XLF)**, **Consumer
  Discretionary (XLY)**, **Industrials (XLI)**, **Real Estate (XLRE)**.
- **Mid cycle** (steady growth, earnings broad, rates neutral): momentum and capex names —
  **Technology (XLK)**, **Communication Services (XLC)**, **Industrials (XLI)**. Often the
  longest, calmest phase.
- **Late cycle** (growth peaking, inflation/rates rising, curve flattening): inflation
  hedges and hard assets — **Energy (XLE)**, **Materials (XLB)**, and staples begin to
  firm as investors turn cautious.
- **Recession** (contraction, rate cuts coming): defensives lead on relative strength —
  **Consumer Staples (XLP)**, **Utilities (XLU)**, **Health Care (XLV)**. They fall less,
  not necessarily rise.

## Risk-on vs risk-off tilt
- **Risk-on (expansion, rising markets):** cyclicals and high-beta — XLK, XLY, XLF, XLI.
  People pay up for growth and discretionary spend.
- **Risk-off (stress, falling markets):** defensives and low-beta — XLP, XLU, XLV. Stable
  cash flows and dividends are prized when growth is scarce.
- **The tell:** watch XLY vs XLP (discretionary over staples = risk-on) and semis vs
  utilities. When defensives quietly outperform in an up market, participation is narrowing.

## The sector ETFs (the rotation dashboard)
- **XLK** Technology · **XLC** Communication Services · **XLY** Consumer Discretionary
- **XLF** Financials · **XLI** Industrials · **XLB** Materials · **XLRE** Real Estate
- **XLE** Energy · **XLP** Consumer Staples · **XLU** Utilities · **XLV** Health Care
- Each is a liquid, low-cost proxy for its sector's breadth — cleaner than a single name.

## Using rotation in practice
- **Relative strength, not absolute.** Rank sector ETFs by 3–6 month return vs the S&P
  (SPY). Leaders keep leading more often than they flip; laggards keep lagging.
- **Confirm the phase.** Cross-check the cycle read against rates, the yield curve, and
  credit spreads (see `macro_regime`) — the tape is the referee, the model is the guess.
- **Tilt, don't bet the farm.** Overweight the phase's leaders modestly; keep a diversified
  core. Rotation calls are frequently early or wrong.
- **Rotation is a source of transient dips.** A quality name can lag simply because its
  sector is out of favour — that's a HOLD signal, not a broken thesis (see `exit_rules`).

## Honest limits
- The cycle rarely announces which phase it's in; phases blur and overlap.
- Central-bank action, shocks, and secular themes (e.g. AI capex) override textbook
  rotation for long stretches.
- ETFs capture the sector's average — individual constituents can diverge sharply.
