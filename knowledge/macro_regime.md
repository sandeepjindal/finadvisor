# Macro Regime

The market's weather. The same stock behaves differently depending on whether capital is
seeking risk or fleeing it. Identify the regime first, then tilt — don't fight the tape.

## Risk-on vs risk-off
- **Risk-on:** growth is trusted, credit spreads tight, volatility (VIX) low, breadth wide.
  Money flows to cyclicals, small caps, high-beta, and long-duration growth.
- **Risk-off:** capital preserves itself — Treasuries, cash, the dollar, defensives, gold.
  Correlations spike (things fall together) and liquidity thins.
- **The tells:** VIX level and trend, credit spreads (HYG/LQD), advance-decline breadth,
  and whether defensives are quietly outperforming in an up market.

## The macro levers and what they shift
- **Interest rates.** Rising rates hurt long-duration growth (Tech, unprofitable names)
  most — future cash flows discount harder — and help Financials (wider margins). Falling
  rates do the reverse and lift rate-sensitive Real Estate and Utilities.
- **Inflation.** High/rising inflation favours real assets — **Energy, Materials**,
  commodities, some staples with pricing power — and punishes fixed-margin, high-multiple
  names. Disinflation is the tailwind for growth and long bonds.
- **The yield curve.** A steep curve signals expansion (good for banks, cyclicals). An
  **inverted curve** (short rates > long) has historically preceded recessions — a caution
  flag that favours a defensive tilt. A curve re-steepening from inversion often precedes
  the actual downturn.
- **Oil.** Rising crude lifts **Energy (XLE)** but taxes consumers and fuel-heavy sectors
  (airlines, transports, discretionary). A crude spike is both an inflation and a growth risk.
- **The dollar (DXY).** A strong dollar pressures multinationals and commodity/EM exposure
  (dollar-priced commodities get pricier abroad); a weak dollar is a tailwind for exporters,
  materials, and international assets.

## Tilting by regime
- **Risk-on / expansion:** overweight cyclicals and growth — Tech (XLK), Discretionary
  (XLY), Financials (XLF), Industrials (XLI); accept more beta.
- **Risk-off / contraction:** overweight defensives — Staples (XLP), Utilities (XLU),
  Health Care (XLV); raise cash, shorten duration, favour quality and dividends.
- **Inflationary / late cycle:** tilt to real assets — Energy, Materials — and pricing-power
  companies; be wary of long-duration multiples.
- **Disinflation / easing:** rotate back toward growth and rate-sensitive sectors as the
  discount rate falls.

## Discipline
- **Regime is a tilt, not an all-in switch.** Keep a diversified core (see `diversification`);
  overweight the regime's winners at the margin.
- **Confirm, don't predict.** The macro read is a hypothesis; let price, breadth, and the
  data confirm it before acting. Regimes turn without warning.
- **Don't fight the Fed or the trend.** Positioning against a clear liquidity/rate regime is
  the most expensive way to be eventually right.
